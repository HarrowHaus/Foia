# Research Operating System

## Research loop

1. **Question:** state a narrow, falsifiable or differentiating question.
2. **Prediction:** write what records should exist if each competing explanation is true.
3. **Discovery:** search multiple independent source families.
4. **Acquisition:** preserve the exact public record and retrieval context.
5. **Extraction:** produce page-level text and flag OCR or parsing uncertainty.
6. **Normalization:** resolve dates, names, organizations, program aliases, identifiers, and locations.
7. **Linkage:** connect claims to exact evidence and counterevidence.
8. **Analysis:** run timeline, network, phrase, duplicate, redaction, and contradiction checks.
9. **Reinterpretation:** revisit prior documents and queries using new vocabulary.
10. **Novelty:** determine whether the result is already known.
11. **Adversarial review:** test ordinary explanations and source dependence.
12. **Checkpoint:** freeze the current state and decide whether to advance, revise, or close.

## Signal classes

- obscure personnel recurring across separate programs;
- institutional or contractor bridges;
- successor programs after formal termination;
- budget discontinuities or transfers;
- synchronized travel, meetings, or facility use;
- terminology migration between agencies;
- cited but missing annexes, attachments, appendices, studies, or subprojects;
- inconsistent redactions across duplicate releases;
- conflicting creation, routing, release, or declassification dates;
- technical concepts appearing before acknowledged adoption;
- repeated language in supposedly independent reports;
- public statements contradicted by contemporaneous internal records;
- records withdrawn or relocated after prior public availability.

## Lead lifecycle

`captured -> triaged -> tested -> corroborated | weakened | blocked | dead_end -> novelty_checked -> publication_candidate`

A lead cannot become a publication candidate without evidence links, competing explanations, and novelty checks.
