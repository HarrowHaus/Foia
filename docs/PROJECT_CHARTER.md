# Project Charter

## Mandate

Discover previously unrecognized facts, relationships, mechanisms, timelines, contradictions, and narratives hidden across already-public records.

## Preferred outcomes

1. A defensible new fact.
2. A previously undocumented institutional or personnel connection.
3. A corrected timeline that materially changes a known account.
4. A reconstructed program, transfer, successor structure, or knowledge pathway.
5. An unexplained contradiction between official records.
6. A scattered documentary sequence that becomes meaningful when assembled.
7. A publishable theory supported by converging independent evidence.
8. An overlooked archive or document family that other researchers can reproduce.

## Primary material

- declassified records and FOIA reading rooms;
- Mandatory Declassification Review releases already online;
- congressional hearings, committee prints, reports, and exhibits;
- inspectors general reports;
- court records and evidentiary exhibits;
- presidential libraries;
- military and intelligence histories;
- procurement, budget, contract, patent, standards, and technical records;
- diplomatic documentary series;
- university special collections with public digital holdings;
- archived government websites;
- public metadata and bulk datasets.

## Primary constraint

The investigation should be capable of meaningful progress from records that are public now. Missing documents may be cataloged, but the project does not stop and wait for new requests.

## Breakthrough standard

A potential breakthrough must have strong provenance, a reproducible trail, converging indicators, historical consequence, a serious novelty case, competing explanations considered, and clearly stated limits.
