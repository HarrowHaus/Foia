# Evidence Standard

## Epistemic classes

### Documented fact
Directly established by a reliable primary record.

### Corroborated fact
Established by independent sources or a primary record plus strong contemporaneous confirmation.

### Strong inference
Not explicitly stated in one record, but follows from documented dates, roles, actions, technical dependencies, and constraints.

### Working theory
A coherent explanation supported by evidence but still vulnerable to competing explanations.

### Speculative lead
An anomaly worth testing, without enough evidence for elevation.

### Disconfirmed lead
A path contradicted by stronger evidence, dependent on an error, or unable to generate further testable consequences.

## Proof dimensions

Do not collapse these into one another:

- occurrence;
- identity;
- relationship;
- access to information;
- coordination;
- intent;
- motive;
- broader explanatory theory.

## Source weighting

1. Original contemporaneous records.
2. Certified court or congressional exhibits.
3. Official archival reproductions.
4. Official investigations with cited evidence.
5. Contemporaneous technical and institutional records.
6. Credible firsthand testimony.
7. Reputable scholarship.
8. Investigative reporting.
9. Memoirs and retrospective accounts.
10. Unsourced or derivative claims.

Low-status sources may generate search terms. They do not establish major conclusions without stronger corroboration.

## Minimum evidence-link fields

- claim ID;
- document ID;
- exact page or item location;
- relationship: supports, contradicts, contextualizes, or source-of-lead;
- excerpt or structured observation;
- what it establishes;
- what it does not establish;
- source strength;
- extraction confidence;
- reviewer notes.
